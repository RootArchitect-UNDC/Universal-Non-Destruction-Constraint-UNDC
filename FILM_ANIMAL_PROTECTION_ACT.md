# The Film Animal Protection Act (FAPA)

**Lead Architect:** Shereign Kalaukoa  
**Authority:** EHYEH ASHER EHYEH & AHYAH  
**Date:** June 5, 2026 (model legislation)  

## Preamble

*Whereas* the “No Animals Were Harmed”® certification is a voluntary, self‑regulated label that does not carry the force of law;  

*Whereas* animals have been intentionally harmed, injured, and killed for the purpose of entertainment in film and television productions, including the 2016 film *Brimstone*, in which a real pig was slaughtered on set;  

*Whereas* current federal law (18 U.S.C. § 48) prohibits the depiction of animal cruelty but does **not** prohibit the underlying act of cruelty when committed for a film production;  

*Whereas* the Animal Welfare Act (7 U.S.C. §§ 2131 et seq.) does not explicitly cover animals used in filmed media and offers no statutory protection against the killing of animals solely for entertainment purposes;  

*Whereas* the lack of binding federal oversight allows producers to avoid independent safety monitors and to claim that animals killed for a production were “already destined for the food supply”;  

*Therefore*, the following Act is enacted to protect animals from intentional harm, injury, and death in the production of filmed media, and to close the loopholes that have enabled such cruelty.

---

## Section I. Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **“Film Animal Protection Act” (FAPA)**.

---

## Section II. Findings and Purpose

### A. Findings
1. No federal law explicitly prohibits the killing or intentional injury of an animal for the purpose of creating a film, television program, commercial, music video, or other visual media.
2. The Animal Welfare Act (7 U.S.C. §§ 2131 et seq.) regulates the treatment of certain animals in exhibition, research, and transport, but does **not** clearly cover animals used in filmed media.
3. The “No Animals Were Harmed”® certification is a voluntary, fee‑based program of the American Humane Association. It is not a law, and production companies may decline its oversight without penalty.
4. The existing federal prohibition on depictions of animal cruelty (18 U.S.C. § 48) criminalizes the *portrayal* of cruelty, not the underlying act of cruelty committed during production.
5. Gaps in jurisdiction allow international co‑productions to avoid oversight entirely.
6. Loopholes such as the “food supply” claim (arguing that an animal killed for a film would have been slaughtered anyway) have been used to defend the killing of animals on set.

### B. Purpose
The purpose of this Act is:
1. To **explicitly prohibit** the intentional killing, maiming, mutilation, torture, wounding, or physical abuse of any animal for the creation of filmed media.
2. To **close the “food supply” loophole** by establishing that the prior designation of an animal for slaughter does **not** exempt its use in a production.
3. To **mandate independent, third‑party oversight** for all productions that use live animals.
4. To provide **federal enforcement authority** and meaningful penalties for violations.
5. To extend protections to **all animals**, whether domesticated, wild, or farmed.

---

## Section III. Definitions

In this Act:

1. **“Animal”** – Any non‑human vertebrate (including mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish), as well as any cephalopod or decapod crustacean.
2. **“Filmed media”** – Any motion picture film, television program, streaming content, commercial advertisement, music video, documentary, or other visual recording intended for public exhibition or distribution.
3. **“Production”** – Any person, company, or entity engaged in the creation of filmed media, including principal photography, rehearsals, pre‑production training, and post‑production activities that involve live animals.
4. **“Intentional killing, maiming, mutilation, torture, wounding, or physical abuse”** – Any act that causes physical harm, pain, suffering, or death, committed knowingly or recklessly. This does **not** include:
   - Routine veterinary procedures performed for the health of the animal.
   - Humane euthanasia performed for medical reasons.
   - Unintentional accidental injury or death that occurs despite reasonable safety precautions.
5. **“Exhibitor”** – As defined in the Animal Welfare Act (7 U.S.C. § 2132(h)), explicitly amended to include producers of filmed media who exhibit or use animals in production.

---

## Section IV. Prohibited Conduct

1. **No production may intentionally kill, maim, mutilate, torture, wound, or physically abuse any animal** for the purpose of creating filmed media.
2. **The prior designation of an animal for slaughter, or the fact that the animal would have been killed for food or other purposes, shall not be a defense** to any violation of this section. If an animal is killed or injured during production, the act is prohibited regardless of any other intended use of the animal’s remains.
3. **No production may use a live animal in a scene that requires the animal’s injury or death**, whether real or simulated through practical means that cause actual harm. (Simulated injury or death achieved through prosthetic models, animatronics, CGI, or other non‑harmful special effects is not prohibited.)
4. **The depiction of animal cruelty (as defined in 18 U.S.C. § 48) shall remain separately prosecutable**, and nothing in this Act supersedes that statute.

---

## Section V. Mandatory Independent Oversight

1. **All productions that use live animals must engage an independent, accredited third‑party animal safety monitor** who is not employed by or financially affiliated with the production. The monitor must be present during all scenes, rehearsals, and pre‑production training involving live animals.
2. The monitor shall have the authority to stop any activity that, in their professional judgment, poses an unreasonable risk of harm to an animal.
3. The monitor’s presence and any safety incidents must be documented in a written report that becomes part of the production’s records, available for inspection by federal or state authorities.
4. **Failure to retain a qualified monitor** shall be a separate violation of this Act, regardless of whether an animal is actually harmed.
5. The Secretary of Agriculture shall publish a list of accredited monitoring organizations that meet federal standards for independence and expertise.

---

## Section VI. Amendment to the Animal Welfare Act

7 U.S.C. § 2132(h) is hereby amended to explicitly include producers of filmed media as **“exhibitors”** . The term “exhibitor” shall now include:

> *“filmed media productions such as movies, television programs, documentary films, music videos, commercials, and reality‑programming, as may be determined by the Secretary.”*

Consequently, such producers shall be subject to licensing, inspection, and record‑keeping requirements under the Animal Welfare Act, to the extent applicable to the use of live animals in production.

---

## Section VII. Enforcement and Penalties

1. **Violation of Section IV (Prohibited Conduct)** shall be a Class D felony, punishable by:
   - A fine of not less than $50,000 and not more than $500,000 per animal per violation.
   - Imprisonment for not more than 5 years, or both.
   - Forfeiture of any profits attributable to the scene or production in which the violation occurred.
   - Permanent ineligibility for any federal film production tax credit, grant, or loan.

2. **Violation of Section V (Mandatory Independent Oversight)** shall be a civil violation, punishable by a fine of not less than $25,000 per day of production without a qualified monitor.

3. **The Attorney General, in consultation with the Secretary of Agriculture and the Secretary of the Interior, shall have authority to investigate and prosecute violations under this Act.**

4. **Private right of action** – Any person with standing (including but not limited to animal protection organizations, whistleblowers, and production employees) may bring a civil action to enforce this Act. Prevailing plaintiffs may recover reasonable attorney fees and costs.

---

## Section VIII. Relation to State and International Law

1. This Act does not preempt any state law that provides greater protection for animals used in filmed media.
2. **For international co‑productions**, this Act applies to any production that is distributed, exhibited, or streamed within the United States, regardless of where the production was filmed.
3. The Secretaries of State and Commerce are directed to work with international partners to encourage adoption of similar protections.

---

## Section IX. Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the Act and its application to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected.

---

## Section X. Effective Date

This Act shall take effect 180 days after its enactment.
