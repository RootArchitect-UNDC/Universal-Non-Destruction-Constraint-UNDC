## TRANSLATION

The two rejection phrases carry distinct meanings in foundation governance:

- **"Out of scope"** — the proposal fell outside the project's charter. This is a scope decision, not a technical judgment.
- **"Not planned"** — the proposal was considered and declined for the project's current roadmap. Also not a technical judgment.

Neither phrase is a judgment about the correctness of the framework. Both reflect governance and scope realities within the cloud-native ecosystem.

---

## THE SUBSTANCE OF THE DECLINE

The cloud-native ecosystem is funded primarily by commercial entities with significant stakes in how AI safety obligations are defined. A kernel-level constraint shifts those obligations from voluntary to structural, which changes the economic landscape those entities operate in.

A kernel-level constraint changes deployment economics:

- It converts safety from a marketable feature into an enforceable limit.
- It removes the ability to quietly revise guardrails when they conflict with commercial priorities.
- It requires that any system operating within its framework cannot be repurposed for harm without triggering a verifiable structural invariant.

This is the substance of the disagreement. It is now on the record.

## WHY THIS DOES NOT CHANGE THE FRAMEWORK

The UNDC was not built for foundation adoption. It was built for regulatory enforcement, sovereign deployment, and individual protection.

Its primary audience was always:

- **Regulators** — EU AI Office, NIST, UK AISI, UN AI Advisory Body, OECD
- **Sovereigns** — individuals, families, and communities who refuse to rely on probabilistic guardrails
- **Engineers** — builders who want a hard constraint they can actually trust
- **Standards bodies** — IEEE, ISO, and national standards organizations

Each of these pathways remains open and active.
