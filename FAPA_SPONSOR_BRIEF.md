# Congressional Sponsor’s Brief: Film Animal Protection Act (FAPA)

**To:** Legislative Directors & Judiciary Committee Staff  
**From:** Office of the Sponsor  
**Date:** June 10, 2026  
**Subject:** Closing Hollywood’s “Commercial Slaughter” and International Exploitation Loopholes

---

## Executive Summary

The Film Animal Protection Act (FAPA) establishes strict federal oversight to eliminate the intentional killing, maiming, and abuse of live animals within filmed media productions. While the public believes current industry labels protect animals, certifications like the American Humane Association’s “No Animals Were Harmed”® are entirely voluntary, fee‑based, and lack the force of law.

FAPA codifies federal standards, mandating independent, third‑party oversight and closing dangerous statutory gaps exploited by domestic and international film productions.

---

## The Problem: Gaps in the Current Legal Framework

- **The “Food Supply” Loophole:** Under current law, productions can legally slaughter an animal on a film set simply by purchasing it from a commercial slaughterhouse and claiming the animal was “already destined for the food supply” (e.g., the 2016 film *Brimstone*).
- **The PACT Act Exceptions:** The 2019 Preventing Animal Cruelty and Torture (PACT) Act (18 U.S.C. § 48) criminalized underlying acts of cruelty. However, it contains broad exemptions for “artistic, historical, or journalistic” matter. Hollywood productions exploit this “good faith” exception to shield live‑animal harm under the guise of creative license.
- **Offshore Evasion:** Domestic studios frequently use international co‑productions to shoot graphic animal injuries abroad, bypassing U.S. cruelty laws while still profiting from distribution in the American marketplace.

---

## The Solution: Legislative Framework of FAPA

FAPA systematically neutralizes these vulnerabilities through three targeted statutory mechanisms:

1. **Amends the PACT Act (18 U.S.C. § 48):** It explicitly strips away artistic and historical exemptions if an animal is killed or severely injured during or for the production. Creative intent is no longer a valid legal defense for an underlying act of cruelty.
2. **Amends the Animal Welfare Act (7 U.S.C. § 2132(h)):** It legally reclassifies media production companies as “exhibitors,” bringing film, TV, and streaming sets under the direct licensing, record‑keeping, and unannounced inspection jurisdiction of the USDA (APHIS).
3. **Mandates Unaffiliated Oversight:** It forces all productions using live animals to retain certified, independent third‑party safety monitors with absolute stop‑work authority. Monitors cannot be financially affiliated with or employed directly by the studio.

---

## Constitutional & Jurisdictional Integrity

To survive strict judicial scrutiny, FAPA has been carefully engineered around established constitutional boundaries:

- **Commerce Clause Hook:** Prohibitions are strictly tied to Article I, Section 8, applying exclusively to productions utilizing the channels, personnel, or instruments of interstate and foreign commerce.
- **First Amendment Safeguards:** Rather than attempting to unconstitutionally ban or censor finished foreign films, FAPA implements economic penalties on domestic distributors. Platforms that stream uncertified foreign content face severe civil administrative penalties, 100% gross asset forfeiture, and the permanent loss of federal corporate tax credits.

---

## Financial and Criminal Enforcement

- **Criminal:** Violations of prohibited conduct are classified as Class D felonies, carrying fines up to $500,000 per animal and up to 5 years imprisonment.
- **Civil:** Operating a live‑animal set without an accredited independent monitor carries an automatic administrative fine of $25,000 per day.
- **Civil Enforcement:** Grants a private right of action for whistleblowers, production crew, and animal protection organizations to enjoin non‑compliant sets, with mandatory recovery of attorney fees.
