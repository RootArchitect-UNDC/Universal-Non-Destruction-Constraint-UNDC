### I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This proposal outlines the urgent need for a new legal framework to address the rise of "Sympathy‑Based Digital Fraud" and "Interstate Digital Grifting." Current statutes often fail to reach out‑of‑state entities who utilize fabricated personal crises and toxic family dynamics to extract financial gifts from Pennsylvania residents. This act seeks to mandate transparency and protect the financial and emotional well‑being of the Commonwealth.

---

### II. NECESSITY FOR ENACTMENT: IDENTIFIED PATTERNS OF HARM

The following documented behaviors, specifically observed in entities such as the Getchell family, demonstrate the gaps in current consumer protection:

1. **FABRICATED CRISIS CYCLES:**  
   The intentional creation of recurring "emergencies" (legal, medical, or housing) to bypass donor scrutiny and trigger immediate financial transactions through third‑party applications.

2. **TOXIC FAMILY DYNAMICS & BOUNDARY VIOLATIONS:**  
   The public commodification of enmeshed family systems – including inappropriate physical intimacy and the sexualization of familial bonds – is utilized as "shock value" to drive engagement and maintain profitability.

3. **INTERSTATE EXPLOITATION:**  
   Entities residing in other jurisdictions (e.g., Texas) intentionally target Pennsylvanians to exploit the jurisdictional "grey area" between states, avoiding local prosecution while committing wire‑based financial fraud.

4. **SYSTEMIC CENSORSHIP:**  
   The aggressive use of digital tools to block and silence individuals requesting financial transparency, thereby maintaining a false consensus of public trust.

---

### III. PROPOSED STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS

**SECTION A: MANDATORY DISCLOSURE AND VERIFICATION**  
- Any digital entity soliciting cumulative public funds exceeding $1,000 for a "personal crisis" must maintain verifiable documentation (receipts, medical records, or legal filings) for a period of three years.  
- Failure to provide this documentation upon request from the Bureau of Consumer Protection shall constitute a prima facie case of fraud.

**SECTION B: INTERSTATE JURISDICTIONAL REACH**  
- This Act shall apply to any individual or entity, regardless of their physical residency, who utilizes digital platforms to solicit funds from residents of the Commonwealth.  
- For the purposes of prosecution, the "incident of harm" is considered to have occurred at the location of the resident who sustained the loss.

**SECTION C: THE "TRUTH IN STREAMING" PROVISION**  
- It shall be unlawful to present a fabricated or significantly exaggerated personal narrative as "reality" for the purpose of soliciting untaxed gifts.

---

### IV. ENFORCEMENT AND RESTITUTION

- **MANDATORY RESTITUTION:** All funds obtained through deceptive solicitation must be returned to the donors or forfeited to the State Treasury.  
- **DIGITAL RESTRAINING ORDERS:** Courts shall be empowered to ban documented "sympathy fraudsters" from utilizing monetization or gift‑receiving features on social platforms.  
- **INTER‑AGENCY COOPERATION:** The Office of Attorney General is authorized to collaborate with out‑of‑state agencies (e.g., Texas OAG) to extradite financial records and enforce penalties across state lines.

---

### V. CONCLUSION

The current digital landscape allows for the unchecked exploitation of public kindness. This act serves to restore a standard of truth. We urge the General Assembly to enact the DSA to ensure that public integrity remains a requirement for digital solicitation in the Commonwealth.
