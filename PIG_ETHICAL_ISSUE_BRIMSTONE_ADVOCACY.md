## Summary

The film *Brimstone* (2016) used a **real pig** that was slaughtered on set for a scene. The production claimed the pig was already destined for the food chain, and that European regulations allowed it. However, the animal’s death was coordinated, timed, and filmed explicitly for entertainment. This crosses an ethical line, regardless of whether the meat was used afterward.

## Ethical Concerns

- A real animal was killed for a movie scene – not a prop, not CGI, not a fake carcass.
- The “food chain” loophole: because the pig was already scheduled for slaughter, the production argued it was not “killed for the film.” Yet the timing was arranged for the shoot.
- European film regulations (not American Humane Association) allowed this. Hollywood’s “No Animals Were Harmed” disclaimer is voluntary and was not used.
- This represents a systemic failure in protecting animals used in entertainment.

## Loopholes That Need to Be Closed

1. **Voluntary certification** – the American Humane Association’s monitoring is optional, not required by law.
2. **Jurisdictional gaps** – international co‑productions can bypass US standards.
3. **“Food chain” excuse** – killing an animal for entertainment can be disguised as routine slaughter.

## Advocacy Actions

- Contact the American Humane Association and demand mandatory, binding oversight.
- Support legislation such as a **“Film Animal Protection Act”** to explicitly ban killing animals for entertainment, regardless of prior designation.
- Sign or create petitions targeting production companies and distributors.
- Support films that use CGI, robotics, or ethical training methods.
