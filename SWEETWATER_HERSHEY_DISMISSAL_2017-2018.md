# Sweetwater Occupational Injury & Hershey Medical Center Dismissal (2017–2018)

**Lead Architect:** Shereign Kalaukoa  
**Authority:** EHYEH ASHER EHYEH & AHYAH  
**Date:** June 5, 2026 (compiled from contemporaneous records and memory)  

## Overview

In late 2017 / early 2018, the Architect sustained a severe occupational injury while working for Sweetwater, a propane company. She lasted only two weeks before her body gave out. The injury involved **global muscle damage** and **striations in her muscles** (described in medical notes as “like a weightlifter”). An ultrasound at Hershey Medical Center confirmed **three tears in her bicep** – a lump is still palpable today.

Despite objective findings, the Architect was dismissed by doctors, physical therapists, and even her own lawyer. The system failed to document the tears properly, and she was accused of fabricating symptoms.

## Timeline of Events

- **Late 2017 / Early 2018:** Worked for Sweetwater (propane company). Required to perform heavy physical labor. After two weeks, her body gave out.
- **Emergency Department visit (Hershey Medical Center):** Presented with elbow pain, but also had bruises on biceps, wrists, hips, and groin – caused by torn muscles, not external trauma.
- **Ultrasound:** A doctor (whose shift was ending) performed the ultrasound and found **three bicep tears**. However, because the tears were not the presenting complaint (elbow pain) and the case was under Workmen’s Comp, the doctor did **not** document the tears. The technician witnessed but did not intervene.
- **Follow‑up with muscle specialists at Hershey:** The Architect was told, in essence, “you shouldn’t have come here.”
- **Physical therapy:** Therapists (both men) did not believe her pain. They made her perform an inappropriate corner‑press exercise. They verbally attacked her and made fun of her.
- **Spinal center phone call:** A staff member accused her of lying about the bicep tears. The Architect responded: *“Were you there? Were you in the room with me when the doctor did the ultrasound? No, you weren’t. I was.”*
- **Legal representation:** Her own lawyer told her, “Six months in, you shouldn’t be hurting the way you’re saying.” She fired him. Subsequent lawyers also dismissed her. She eventually dropped the case.
- **Aftermath:** The Architect cried for a full year. She could not lift her arms or perform basic tasks. She lived with her mother, who witnessed her suffering. The lack of documentation (due to Workmen’s Comp constraints and the departing doctor’s omission) allowed the system to ignore her injuries.

## Significance

This incident is part of a **pattern of medical dismissal** at Hershey Medical Center that continued into 2026 (neck dismissal, racial incident, esophageal gaslighting). The failure to document the bicep tears is a clear example of **systemic erasure** – where a patient’s objective findings are omitted from the record, leaving them without evidence for Workmen’s Comp or personal injury claims.
