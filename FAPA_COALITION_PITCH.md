## 1. Purpose of This Proposal

We invite your organizations to formally endorse and co‑champion the **Film Animal Protection Act (FAPA)** , a model federal bill engineered to eliminate intentional animal slaughter, injury, and exploitation in filmed media. While existing voluntary frameworks offer the illusion of safety, structural loopholes leave animals completely unprotected from calculated commercial harm under the guise of artistic expression. FAPA provides the binding statutory teeth necessary to close these gaps permanently.

---

## 2. The Urgent Policy Gaps

Despite the widespread public belief that the industry is heavily regulated, two critical legal blind spots persist:

- **The “Food Supply” Exploitation:** Production companies currently bypass animal cruelty laws by purchasing animals from commercial slaughterhouses and killing them on active sets. Because the animal was “already destined for the food supply,” filmmakers escape prosecution by claiming no net increase in harm occurred (as seen on the set of the film *Brimstone*).

- **Voluntary Oversight Flaws:** Industry‑standard certifications are fee‑based, voluntary programs. Film productions can simply refuse oversight without a single statutory penalty.

---

## 3. Core Statutory Solutions

FAPA systematically reforms federal animal welfare standards through three targeted mechanisms:

1. **Neutralizes the Slaughter Loophole (Section IV(2)).** – Explicitly states that an animal’s prior designation for food supply or commercial slaughter is not a valid legal defense if the animal is killed or harmed during or for a media production.

2. **Amends the PACT Act (18 U.S.C. § 48).** – Strips away the historical “artistic, historical, or journalistic” exceptions if the underlying cruelty occurred dynamically for the purpose of creating the media. Creative intent can no longer shield a criminal act.

3. **Amends the Animal Welfare Act (7 U.S.C. § 2132(h)).** – Legally reclassifies media producers as “exhibitors,” placing them under the direct licensing, inspection, and enforcement jurisdiction of the USDA (APHIS).

---

## 4. Constitutional Integrity

This legislation has been rigorously built to survive corporate legal pushback:

- **Commerce Clause Alignment:** All provisions are firmly anchored to interstate commerce channels, equipment transport, and digital streaming networks.

- **First Amendment Defensibility:** Rather than attempting to unconstitutionally ban or censor completed international films, FAPA implements economic and civil asset forfeitures on the domestic distributors who profit from uncertified foreign animal cruelty.
